# AR-1417: Interactive stateful literature workloads

## Objective

Make established stateful, interactive literature workloads selectable as workload
IDs beside the built-in software-engineering suite, without flattening them into
patch tasks or claiming upstream qualification.

## Dependencies and scope

- Depends on AR-1416 (complete selector and deterministic local/mock lifecycle).
- Depends on AR-1408 (closed provenance and methodology boundaries).
- Product repository only; do not modify `asb-tui`.
- Owned paths: literature registry records, workload adapter/catalog plumbing,
  public docs, fixtures, and focused tests.

## Acceptance

1. Audit `docs/RELATED_WORK.md`, `docs/WORKLOADS.md`, and the pinned literature
   sources. Add a stable record for AgentBench's stateful OS/database classes and
   every other qualifying stateful workload named there, or a durable exclusion
   with its reason, license, source revision, and evaluator boundary.
2. Preserve task protocol, environment, grader, dataset, and adaptation identities;
   records distinguish executable, methodology-only, unavailable, and provenance-only
   states and fail closed when evaluator/image/reset evidence is absent.
3. Add bounded public fixtures for state transitions, tool calls, malformed actions,
   timeout, reset/restart, grader disagreement, and denied egress. No fixture embeds
   credentials, private data, upstream task secrets, or raw transcripts.
4. `plan`, `run`, `sweep`, `record`, `replay`, and `report` expose these IDs beside
   built-in workloads; unsupported or methodology-only records cannot execute and
   explain the exact missing qualification.
5. Add positive and hostile tests for catalog parity, duplicate/unknown IDs,
   stale revisions, state-reset isolation, bounded evidence, and local/mock-only
   execution. Run focused and full workspace gates, exact-head CI, independent
   review, and all post-merge assurance workflows.

## Non-goals

No live provider connection, network acquisition, privileged container, native
platform claim, score pooling with software-engineering tasks, or new runtime dependency.
